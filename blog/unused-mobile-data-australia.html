What Happens to Unused Mobile Data in Australia?
(And How to Stop Losing It)

📅 June 2026 ⏱ 6 min read 🇦🇺 Australian data

Every month, millions of Australians pay for mobile data they never use — and at the end of the billing cycle, it simply disappears. No refund. No rollover. No warning. It just expires. This article explains exactly why that happens, how much it's costing you, and what's changing.

Why Does Mobile Data Expire at the End of Each Month?

When you sign up for a mobile plan, you're not buying data the way you buy a bottle of water. You're buying access to a network for a set period — and the data allowance attached to that plan is designed to expire when the period ends.

This is a deliberate commercial structure, not a technical necessity. The telco's network doesn't "run out" when your billing cycle resets. The infrastructure keeps running. Your 60GB allowance vanishing on the 30th isn't a physical event — it's a contractual one.

Telcos set plans this way for a few reasons: it encourages customers to buy larger plans "just in case," it prevents customers from banking large allowances over time, and it makes revenue predictable month-to-month. It's entirely legal — it's in the fine print — but it means that most Australians systematically overpay for data they never use.

The buffer plan problem: Most Australians are on a plan bigger than they need. The fear of running out mid-month costs real money every single billing cycle. If you're on a 60GB plan and regularly use 10–15GB, you're losing the equivalent of 3–4 plans' worth of data every month.

Does Any Mobile Data Roll Over in Australia?

A small number of Australian mobile plans offer data rollover — allowing unused data to carry forward to the next billing cycle. Optus has offered rollover on some plans, and a handful of MVNOs (smaller carriers that piggyback on major networks) have experimented with it.

However, rollover has significant limitations:

  • It typically only carries over for one billing cycle — unused data from cycle 1 doesn't survive past cycle 2.
  • It's not available on most plans, and carriers can remove it at any time.
  • Rolled-over data is usually capped, so you can't accumulate large balances.
  • It still doesn't compensate you for data that ultimately goes unused after the rollover period.

For the majority of Australian mobile subscribers, data simply expires at month's end with no compensation and no recourse.

How Much Unused Mobile Data Are Australians Wasting?

The scale is staggering. According to the ACCC Communications Market Report 2024–25, the average Australian mobile subscriber wastes approximately 55GB per month — data they paid for but never used.

Across Australia's 26 million mobile subscribers, that adds up to:

  • 1.43 billion GB of data wasted nationally every month
  • 17.16 billion GB wasted every year
  • $9.9 billion in consumer value lost annually

To put that in perspective — $9.9 billion is roughly the same as the entire annual revenue of some of Australia's largest retailers. It disappears silently, every month, across millions of phone plans.

Your personal waste: On an average 60GB plan, if you use 14GB per month (the national average usage figure), you're wasting 46GB — roughly $23 worth of data every single month at standard retail rates. That's $276 per year.

Is It Legal for Telcos to Let Data Expire?

Yes — under current Australian consumer law, it is entirely legal for telcos to expire your unused data at the end of a billing cycle. The terms are disclosed in your plan agreement, and the ACCC has not classified expiring data as an unfair contract term.

That said, there is growing awareness of the issue. Consumer advocacy groups, industry researchers, and independent media have increasingly highlighted the structural nature of data waste in Australian telecommunications. The ACCC's own reporting documents the scale of the problem in detail.

The situation isn't illegal — but that doesn't mean it's fair, or that consumers are powerless to do anything about it.

What Can You Actually Do About Unused Mobile Data?

There are a few practical steps Australians can take right now:

1. Right-size your plan

Check your actual data usage in your phone's settings or your telco's app. If you're consistently using 15GB on a 60GB plan, consider stepping down. You'll save on your monthly bill and waste less.

2. Use Wi-Fi more deliberately

If you're burning through your allowance on streaming or downloads you could do over Wi-Fi, switching to Wi-Fi at home and work extends your mobile data further.

3. Look for rollover plans

Check whether your current carrier offers any form of data rollover. Some MVNOs — smaller carriers like Boost, Woolworths Mobile, and Kogan — offer more flexible terms than the big three.

DBUs are classified as non-monetary rewards credits (similar to frequent flyer points or supermarket loyalty rewards), not cash or financial products. They can be used in the Urbyte rewards marketplace, donated to charity through the Givabit program, or held in your GBank data vault.
